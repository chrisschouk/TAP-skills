# Pitch drafter

A pitch is a set of cited facts arranged so a busy person can say yes.

## When to use

- A pitch is due to a named outlet, contact, or curator.
- A draft contains a claim nobody can source.
- A follow-up is due on a pitch already sent.

## Preflight, before writing a word

1. **The contact.** Confirm the person still works there and has not asked to be
   left alone. If the contact is stale or cooling off, stop and say so.
2. **The links.** Open every link in the draft. A dead link is worse than no link.
3. **The facts.** For each claim, attach `[src: X]`. If it cannot be cited, delete
   it. Do not soften it, do not hedge it, delete it.
4. **The missing fields.** If the label, the release date, or the genre is unknown,
   omit it. Never fill a gap with a plausible guess.

## Procedure

1. **One line on why this outlet.** Reference something they actually published.
   Not "I love your work".
2. **One line on the artist.** Who they are, in language the outlet uses.
3. **One line on why now.** The release date, the news hook, the moment.
4. **The assets.** Link to the track, the bio, the photo. Named, not dumped.
5. **The ask.** One sentence. A review, a feature, a playlist add. Pick one.

## Guardrails

- No invented label names, quotes, statistics, or client relationships.
- No attachments without a named reason.
- No mass send. One pitch, one outlet, one ask.
- If the pitch needs a fact you do not have, stop and ask. Do not write around it.

## Output

The pitch, a citation list for every claim, and a preflight line confirming the
contact and links were checked.
